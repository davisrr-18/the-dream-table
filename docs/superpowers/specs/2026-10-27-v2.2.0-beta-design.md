# The Dream Table 2.2.0-beta Design

## Intent

Make The Dream Table substantially more reliable and easier to operate, especially on multiplayer servers. The release combines a deliberate stabilization pass, a complete but compatible menu redesign, and server-authoritative cooperative farm management.

## Product goals

- Ship with no known Critical or High defects and no known Medium defects in flows changed by 2.2.
- Replace the nested, narrow menu with a refined Minecraft-style adaptive interface that remains safe around handled inventory slots.
- Let an owner share a finished farm with accepted Viewers or Operators without transferring ownership or exposing unrelated farm data.
- Give authorized players persistent alerts, favorites, tracking, and clear farm status without remotely transporting items.
- Preserve old worlds, finished layouts, active sessions, page ids, and Keep Loaded selections.

## Non-goals

- Ownership transfer, public farms, cross-server identity, remote item collection or transport, and automatic repair.
- Theme selection, complex animation, card drag-and-drop, or graphical dashboards.
- Claiming that no undiscovered bugs exist. The release gate applies to known, reproduced findings.

## Quality policy

Maintain `docs/quality/v2.2.0-beta-bugs.md` with severity, reproduction, expected and actual behavior, cause, regression coverage, and resolution. Critical findings include save/world corruption, broad loss or duplication, unauthorized execution, and recurring server crashes. High findings include lost progress/items, role bypasses, unrecoverable persisted operations, and crashes in common flows. Medium findings are incorrect flows with a workaround. Critical and High findings block the release everywhere; Medium findings block it in 2.2-touched flows. Low or cosmetic findings may be recorded for later unless the UI polish directly touches them.

## Interface design

The visual language remains pixel-aligned blue and cream, using vanilla item icons and semantic green, amber, red, and grey accents. State must never be communicated by color alone.

There are two deterministic layouts:

- Compact: 196 logical pixels when the scaled screen width is below 420.
- Wide: a 384-pixel panel at 420 pixels or wider.

The panel height is at most `screenHeight - 8`. Header, primary tabs, contextual navigation, and search stay fixed; only page content scrolls. Handled slot coordinates remain immutable. Both layouts retain a 196-pixel slot region and calculate panel, content, and slot origins separately. Wide pages may use two 168-pixel columns or a main column plus context, while compact pages stack the same content.

Four persistent primary tabs replace hidden root navigation:

- Build: catalog, category, Work, Requirements, Materials, and Swap.
- Farms: Overview, Alerts, Invites, and farm details.
- Schematics: imported and rejected designs; selecting one enters Build.
- Settings: build speed, Show Other Farms, tracker visibility, and client preferences.

Build opens active work when one exists and otherwise the catalog. Other tabs always open their roots. Existing `PAGE_*` meanings remain stable; new ids do not collide. In compact mode tabs show icons with tooltip and narration; in wide mode they show icon and text. Badges cap visually at `99+`.

Shared UI components cover tabs, breadcrumbs, section labels, cards, badges, primary/secondary/destructive/icon buttons, loading/empty/error states, inline result feedback, confirmations, progress, chips, search, and scrolling. Disabled controls expose a reason. Destructive actions use an explicit Cancel/Confirm state.

Keyboard behavior is part of the contract: Tab and Shift+Tab traverse controls, horizontal arrows traverse tabs, vertical arrows traverse lists, Enter/Space activates, and Escape goes back one level before closing a root. Focus moves to the first valid control on page change, remains on the same stable control after refresh when possible, and scrolls into view. Every control exposes a narrated label and state.

Slots are interactive only on their intended page. Draw, highlight, tooltip, and hit testing use the same slot origin. Clipped slots are not clickable, switching tabs with a cursor stack cannot lose or duplicate it, and shift-click remains restricted to valid destinations.

## Farm identity and access

Every finished farm owns an immutable `instanceId` UUID. Its public key is `dimension|instanceId`; list position, table position, and site are not identities. Old saves receive and persist a UUID on read. Old Keep Loaded keys migrate when their positional farm is resolved. Reusing a site never inherits metadata from the removed farm.

Farm-owned metadata is saved with its finished record: owner, optional custom name, accepted grants, pending invitations, operation state, active alerts, and bounded history. A separate overworld persistent state stores per-player favorites, tracked farm, and alert acknowledgement cursors.

Roles are:

- Owner: all actions, including rename, grants, Take Down, and Forget.
- Operator: view, favorite, track, Repair, and toggle the owner's Keep Loaded selection.
- Viewer: view, favorite, track, and read alerts.

All reads and mutations resolve the stable key on the server and derive the effective role from authenticated UUIDs. The client never supplies a trusted owner or role. Responses reveal only accessible farms and return an explicit result: `SUCCESS`, `NOT_FOUND`, `FORBIDDEN`, `LIMIT_REACHED`, `EXPIRED`, `BUSY`, or `INVALID_INPUT`.

An owner can invite an online or server-known profile as Viewer or Operator. Invitations require acceptance, expire after seven real days, reject self/duplicate grants, and use UUID for authority with a stored name only as display fallback. Defaults are eight accepted collaborators per farm and 32 pending incoming invitations per player. Reducing limits never deletes existing data. Sharing can be disabled without deleting grants; disabled grants are ineffective.

Custom farm names are global to authorized viewers, non-unique, trimmed, at most 32 characters, and reject control characters and `§`. Empty input restores the design name. Favorites are individual.

Server properties are `farm_sharing_enabled=true`, `max_collaborators_per_farm=8`, and `max_pending_invites_per_player=32`.

## Cooperative operations

Keep Loaded selections and quotas remain owned by the farm owner. An Operator toggle mutates that owner's selection atomically. When offline loading is disabled, an accepted online Operator counts as authorized presence; a Viewer does not. Shared viewers see the selected farm's state and `Owner quota full`, never the owner's other selections.

Repair distinguishes `farmOwnerUuid` from `actorUuid`. The owner controls farm identity, grants, quota, final record, and history. The actor controls the open table/session, online wait, materials, territorial permission checks, refunds, and messages. A normal build uses the same UUID for both.

The finished record remains present during Repair and persists `READY` or `REPAIRING` plus an operation id. Conflicting mutations are rejected while repairing. Restart resumes without changing ownership; an orphaned operation safely returns to READY and records failure. Revocation prevents new work but does not cancel an already authorized repair. Per-step land permissions continue to apply.

Minecart repair handles the vehicle and passenger independently. An intact correct pair is skipped; a lone correct passenger receives a new vanilla cart; a lone empty vanilla cart receives a new passenger; neither creates the pair. Occupied carts and passengers in other vehicles are not reused. Matching is cell-local and deterministic. Partial failure removes only entities created by that attempt.

## Farms hub, alerts, and tracking

Farm Overview supports search, access/status/dimension/loading filters, and attention/distance/name/date sorting. Cards show custom or design name, role, favorite, health, storage, missing blocks, and distance/dimension. Detail actions are role-aware.

Active alert types are `STORAGE_FULL`, `BLOCKS_MISSING`, `DESIGN_UNAVAILABLE`, `REPAIR_BLOCKED`, and `REPAIR_FAILED`; `REPAIR_COMPLETED` is history-only. There is at most one active alert of a type per farm and 20 resolved/informational history entries per farm. Count changes update an active alert rather than creating duplicates. Unknown state from unloaded chunks never resolves an alert. The server pages up to 50 alerts with active entries first. The client acknowledges only displayed event ids; read problems remain active until resolved. A newly accepted collaborator sees active alerts and history created after acceptance. New problem chat goes to online Owners and Operators only and is not replayed at login.

One accessible farm may be tracked per player. In the same dimension the HUD shows name, direction, and distance; in another it shows name and dimension without misleading direction. It renders top-center below boss bars, hides with F1, and has a client visibility setting. Revocation, removal, or stale identity clears tracking and related orphaned preferences.

## Compatibility and verification

NBT additions use new optional keys and safe defaults. Existing page ids, session keys, layout ids, and schematic behavior remain compatible. Network strings and collections have explicit bounds; all C2S handlers revalidate the open Dream Table, distance/use permission, stable key, and current role.

Automated coverage includes legacy migration and NBT round trips, forged role actions, restart during repair, concurrent operations, owner quota with an online Operator, invitation expiry/limits, alert transitions and privacy, minecart partial states, pure layout geometry, hit regions, clipping, and focus order.

Manual release validation covers scaled sizes 320×240, 416×240, 420×240, 640×360, and 854×480; empty/loading/error/long lists; maximum names and materials; build states; rejected schematics; badge counts 0/1/99+; all roles; destructive confirmations; cursor stacks; shift-click; keyboard; mouse; scroll; drag; refresh; and resize. In-game screenshots are required because geometric unit tests do not prove visual quality.
